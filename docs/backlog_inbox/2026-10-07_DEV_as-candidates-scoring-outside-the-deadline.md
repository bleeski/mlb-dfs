# 2026-10-07 DEV (Session 134, R465(a)): `as_candidates` scores every served candidate after the bank's slice, outside both the search budget and the publication reserve

**Observed (read in the tree at ebf9333; found by the advisor's question at the D6 measurement, then read).** On the sliced door `run_classic` sizes the bank's search from `deadline.slice(label="sliced bank")` (`build_slate.py`, `resolve_bank_budget`), which is the whole spendable window up to `end - reserve`. After the bank is built it calls `deadline.mark("sliced_bank_built")` and THEN `cache.as_candidates(projections, requested_n=n_entries, contest_shapes=slice_shapes)`, which runs `score_lineup_candidate` once for the base mode and once per contest shape for every served candidate. The publication reserve (`repo_env.PUBLICATION_RESERVE_MEASURED`, `deadline.Deadline.reserve_s`) was measured on "one 734-candidate bank" and covers what `run_slate` spends AFTER a bank is in hand ("rebuilds the frame, runs the allocator's joint MILP, certifies, exports and writes": p95 1.62 s at 9 entries, 5.84 s at 38, 40.46 s at 150). The bank-size ceiling's comment (`repo_env.py`, above `BANK_CANDIDATES_PER_ENTRY_REFERENCE`: "the bank costs a linear ~0.165s per candidate", measured on the 2026-06-28 11g slate, 38 entries, 630 s container) reads as the job-solve cost and nothing in it says it includes scoring. Nothing in either table is a scoring figure.

**Why it matters.** Measured on benbook, Windows, under CPU load (a synthetic 240-player frame, three shapes): about 54 ms per candidate, so 83 s at the 1,536 ceiling and 163 s at 3,072 (15.4% and 30.2% of the host's 540 s call budget; see `2026-10-07_DEV_served-set-filter-with-topup.md` for the table and its caveats). On a build whose bank search consumed its whole slice, that scoring lands inside the publication reserve (5.84 s p95 at 38 entries), so the build can overshoot the deadline by tens of seconds with nothing in the brief saying where the time went (`build_deadline` stages `sliced_bank_built` to the next mark). Whether it does on a given host depends on whether the search stops on the candidate cap (cap-bound builds leave slack) or on the clock (clock-bound builds do not). It is independent of R465's union: the single-bucket case at the ceiling already costs 15%.

**Not measured here (the 10-minute box was spent).** The same call on the cloud container (a different CPU), on a real `runs/bank_cache_*.json` (Ben's disk), and unloaded (the measurement above ran beside a three-suite test run).
